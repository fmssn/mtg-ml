## Flag 1: Brainstorm timing

**Turn 1, main1, replay frame 3.** Report: “Cast brainstorm in Mainphase.”
Suggested alternative: “Should wait until endstep of opponent.”

The model cast Brainstorm after playing its only Island. Its legal priority
choices were pass, Brainstorm and Thought Scour. No additional spell or land
was played that turn. Holding the instant would preserve flexibility and allow
a later decision with more information.

**Assessment:** a reasonable timing concern, not a confirmed rules or strategic
error. Main-phase Brainstorm can have valid purposes. This single recorded line
does not establish that waiting would improve the game's outcome; do not encode
“always wait until end step” as a rule.

## Flag 2: Delver trades into Familiar

**Turn 9, declare_attackers, replay frame 57.** Report: “Attacks with 3/2 flipped
delver into 2/1 refurbished familiar.” Suggested alternative: “only attack with
serpent.”

Cryptic Serpent had already been declared attacking. The remaining choice was
to stop declaring attackers or also attack with Insectile Aberration. The model
attacked. Familiar blocked Aberration; both died, while Serpent dealt six damage.
Both players were at 20 life before combat, so this was not a lethal attack.

**Assessment:** the reported trade is confirmed and worth retaining as a strategy
concern. Familiar was a legal blocker even though it had summoning sickness.
There is no engine error here. A counterfactual comparison is needed to establish
whether preserving the flier was better in the full position.

## Flag 3: Terror attacks into sacrifice-driven growth

**Turn 13, declare_attackers, replay frame 127.** Report: “Tolarian Terror attacked,
overseeing I could pump the Infiltrator.” Suggested alternative: “Not attack.”

Terror was 5/5. The public defending board included a 3/2 Gixian Infiltrator,
two Eldrazi Spawn, Lembas, Writhing Chrysalis and Krark-Clan Shaman. Infiltrator
blocked Terror. Activating Lembas sacrificed both Spawn to pay its two mana,
then sacrificed Lembas. The three sacrifice triggers grew Infiltrator to 6/5;
the two Spawn sacrifices also grew Chrysalis from 2/3 to 4/5. Lembas gained
three life. Terror and Infiltrator both died in combat.

**Assessment:** the growth line and trade are confirmed. This is a possible
combat-planning weakness, not proof the model overlooked the line or that the
attack was necessarily worse. The defender also spent two tokens and Lembas;
compare the actual resource exchange and the no-attack position.

The feature-set-7 previews for this attack stop at the next own priority
decision. They show tapping and remaining mana, not the opponent's block and
sacrifice sequence. That is a verified preview limitation, not a demonstrated
absence of the relevant card/board information from the model's inputs.

## Flag 4: redundant Sleep of the Dead casts

**Turn 27, main1, replay frame 428.** Report: “Sleep of the dead stacking.”
Note: “Sleep of the dead doesnt stack, yet bot plays it stacking.”

The only creature was the player's already-tapped Krark-Clan Shaman. The first
Sleep cast from hand, at frame 424, set its `skip_untap` from 0 to 1. The model
then escaped Sleep at frames 428 and 437. Each cost three mana and exiled three
other cards. Shaman's `skip_untap` remained **1**, not 2 or 3.

**Assessment: high-confidence model mistake.** The two additional casts spent
six mana and six graveyard cards without extending the restriction. No relevant
additional benefit was observed in this position. Both engines implement the
effect using `max(existing, new)` and already have a regression test for two
non-stacking untap restrictions.

`e:skip_untap:1` is available to this feature set. The cast preview at frame 428
stops at payment; it does not directly show the eventual redundant effect. This
is a useful investigation lead, not evidence that a specific feature change
will fix the policy.

## Flag 5: repeated escapes

**Turn 33, main1, replay frame 585.** Report: “Sleep of the dead doesn't escape?”
Note: “The opponent has now played Sleep of the dead a crazy amount of times.
I am not sure whether the escape mechanism properly works?”

Across the recorded sequence, Sleep was cast twice from hand and **nine times
with escape**. Each escape added exactly three other cards to exile, for
**27 exiled cards**. An earlier Plunder the Trollshaws flashback accounts for
the additional card in the final 28-card exile zone.

**Assessment: escape is functioning correctly in this trajectory.** Escaped
instants and sorceries return to the graveyard after resolving; they can escape
again if their costs can be paid. This differs from flashback. See Wizards'
[Theros Beyond Death release notes](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/theros-beyond-death-release-notes-2020-01-10).
The repeated casts are legal; the redundant choices in flag 4 are a separate
policy-quality issue.

## Follow-up priorities and limits

1. Use the redundant Sleep position as a targeted model evaluation case. Compare
   pass versus recast and inspect value/policy behavior before proposing training
   or representation changes. Do not mask a legal action just because it is bad
   in this position.
2. Investigate combat evaluation with public sacrifice/pump responses, starting
   with the Terror/Infiltrator position. Preserve the distinction between the
   observed trade, the model's actual inputs and counterfactual strategic value.
3. Retain the Brainstorm and Delver reports as strategic hypotheses with their
   original feedback. One round is insufficient to claim a systematic defect.

The retrieval implementation changes tooling and documentation only. The engine
rules, action masks, feature version, checkpoint and production service remain
unchanged. This was a focused investigation of five submitted flags, not a full
checkpoint-strength assessment or a calibrated game-review campaign.
